Purpose: keep the paper honest. Each claim below has a status, evidence, and allowed wording.

## Claim Status Key

- **Supported**: current artifacts directly support the claim at pilot scale.
- **Conditional**: supported only under a stated condition.
- **Weak / exploratory**: useful signal, not enough for a main claim.
- **Not supported**: must not be claimed.

## Main Claim For Drafts

Use this wording:

> Naive directed disconfirmation fails under negative controls. The stronger result is a validity-aware selective-compute policy: classify whether the question is answerable, false-premise, or ambiguous, then allocate test-time compute to direct answering, clarification, presupposition checking, retrieval, retrieval-backed premise checking, verification, or disconfirmation. In small local pilots, routing and verification improve decision quality while neutral reconsideration remains a strong control for challenge wording.

## Next Claim That Needs Real Evidence

Do not claim this yet:

> A general retrieval-backed event verifier improves selective QA on open-domain false-premise questions.

Evidence needed:

- 240-300 item held-out benchmark, or a justified 240-item minimum run;
- source selection over a noisy open corpus;
- source-selection accuracy;
- verifier accuracy conditional on correct source;
- end-to-end route accuracy;
- comparison against neutral reconsideration and ordinary retrieval QA.
